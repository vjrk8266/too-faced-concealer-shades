## Why Super Coverage is the one most people mean

It's a liquid, full-coverage concealer with a natural finish, and the pitch is that it's hydrating rather than matte and cakey. The brand-loaded ingredients are coconut water, alpine rose and hyaluronic acid. Sephora's listing flags it as oil-free and non-comedogenic, and the wear claim is 12 hours.

Three things make it flexible enough to justify the price for regular users:

- It's marketed as a 4-in-1 — concealer, contour, highlight and retouch, all from the same bottle. You're not buying separate products for each job.
- It layers over and under other makeup, so it can go on after foundation without dragging it around.
- The finish is described as photo-friendly with no flashback, which matters if you're being photographed under flash lighting.

### What the diamond applicator is actually for

The applicator isn't a gimmick, and the brand's own usage guidance is specific:

1. **Conceal under eyes or hyperpigmentation** — place a dot from the flat side of the diamond directly on the area and blend with fingers, a brush, or a sponge using a pressing motion. One dot is enough; the formula builds.
2. **Conceal blemishes** — use the tip for precision, not the flat side.
3. **Contour** — pick a neutral or cool-toned shade roughly three tones darker than your skin tone. That gives you shadow instead of a smear.
4. **Highlight** — pick a shade two to three tones lighter than your skin, in the same undertone, and place it on the high points of the face.
5. **Retouch** — one dot pressed anywhere your makeup looks tired refreshes the whole base.

That contour and highlight guidance is the part most people skip, and it's the reason some buyers end up with a concealer that looks grey under the eyes: they bought a cool shade three tones too light and are using it for both jobs.

## Brighten & Correct is not a backup concealer

At US$17 for 0.06 oz, this one is easy to file under "extra stuff I don't need." It isn't a coverage product in the same sense — it's a colour corrector meant to be layered *beneath* foundation and concealer. Six shades, built specifically for under-eye discolouration.

The logic: if what you're covering is blue-purple or brownish pigmentation, piling on more concealer gets you a heavier undereye, not a brighter one. Correcting the colour first and covering lightly after is the standard approach.

That said, it changes your routine from one product to two. A full-size concealer plus the corrector is US$53 before tax, and if your undereye issue is mild, a well-matched concealer used correctly does the job on its own. The corrector earns its money when you've already tried the single-product route and the grey cast is still there.

## Full size or the US$16 travel size?

Run the actual maths before you default to the big bottle. The full size is 0.45 fl oz at US$36, which works out to roughly US$80 per fluid ounce. Sephora's listing for the mini shows a 0.06 oz / 2 mL size at US$16 — about US$237 per fluid ounce. Read that size twice before comparing, because mini volumes vary by listing and retailer, but the direction is clear: the small one is expensive per millilitre on purpose.

Two honest reasons to still buy the mini:

- You want to confirm the formula works on your skin before committing to a bottle that will take a year to finish.
- You don't wear concealer often. A mini that lasts months beats a full size that separates at the bottom of a drawer.

One dot covers an undereye and one blemish. If that's your daily usage, the full size is a genuinely long-term purchase.

## Getting the shade right the first time

Thirty shades, split across warm, neutral and cool undertones, with names that describe the shade rather than the depth — Cloud is fairest neutral to rosy, Cream Puff is very fair rosy, Light Beige is light with neutral undertones, Golden Beige is light golden, Honey is tan neutral, Butterscotch, Caramel and Mocha sit deeper, and Cookie reads as medium golden to olive.

Shade naming is not consistent across Too Faced's lines, and the same name can carry a different undertone in a different product family. Which means a "Natural Beige" you loved in one formula isn't automatically the right Natural Beige here.

> If you can get to a counter, swatch on the jaw and cheek and look at it in daylight. If you're buying blind, buy the conceal shade to match your skin and the highlight shade separately — don't try to make one shade do both.

Returns exist at the major retailers, but they carry a real cost if you're buying through a cashback route: a returned or refunded order isn't eligible for cashback, and partial returns get prorated down. Getting the shade right the first time is worth more than the flexibility of returning it.

## When Too Faced concealers actually get cheaper

The concealers sit close to full price most of the year. The discount windows are predictable, which means you can plan around them instead of buying on impulse.

- **Beauty Pass sale windows.** Sephora runs member sale periods roughly twice a year, with tier-linked discounts in the region of 10 to 20 percent depending on membership tier in Singapore's programme. Australia's Beauty Pass structure puts anchor sale months around April, September and November, plus members-only Beauty Pass Weekend windows.
- **8.8, 9.9 and 11.11.** These campaign days are when several beauty retailers discount concurrently, which is exactly when a stacked cashback rate is worth most.
- **Year-end holiday.** Sephora's biggest combined window, usually running into Black Friday and Boxing Day.
- **Gift-with-purchase.** Too Faced regularly rides along on retailer GWPs rather than straight discounts. A free deluxe sample isn't a price cut, but it's real value if you were going to buy the thing anyway.

What to skip: anything already on a clearance rack. Sitewide vouchers often don't stack on marked-down stock, and third-party marketplace sellers create both authenticity and cashback-tracking problems on a product where counterfeits are common.

## Questions that come up before buying

**Which Too Faced concealer is best for dark circles?**
If the discolouration reads blue or purple, the Brighten & Correct layer underneath does more than extra concealer on top. If it's mild, a correctly matched Super Coverage shade used with the official dot-and-press method is enough on its own.

**Is it safe for acne-prone skin?**
Sephora's listing flags the formula as non-comedogenic and oil-free, which are the two claims worth checking on any concealer if you break out easily.

**How long does the coverage last?**
The wear claim across retailer listings is 12 hours. That's a brand figure, not an independent test result — treat it as the target, not a guarantee.

**Can I use it on the Sephora app and still earn cashback?**
No. App purchases are excluded from cashback, and this is one of the most common reasons claims fail.

**Is one bottle enough for contour and highlight?**
Physically one bottle is enough product. Practically you need two shades — a neutral-to-cool shade about three tones darker for contour, and a shade two to three lighter in your own undertone for highlight.

**Does ShopBack work in the US?**
No. It's available across Asia-Pacific markets and Germany, not the United States.
